# Play Store listing copy

Everything here is plain text on purpose. The previous listing used Unicode
bold letters (𝐌𝐞𝐝𝐢𝐜𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐈𝐝𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐢𝐟𝐢𝐞𝐫) — screen readers read those as
gibberish, and Play's search may not index them as words. Never paste styled
characters into these fields.

Character limits are Play's and are hard: the Console refuses to save over
them. The counts below were measured, not estimated.

## Full description — 4000 characters max

```
Open any bathroom cabinet and you will find a box printed in a language you do not read, an ingredient name nobody recognises, and no leaflet. The questions are always the same four: What is this for? How much do I take? Can I take it with food? What happens if it disagrees with me?

Medication Identifier answers them from a photograph of the box, in about two seconds.

POINT YOUR CAMERA AT THE BOX
The name is read straight off the packaging. No typing, no squinting at small print. Works on a box, a blister strip, or a bottle label. If it is not a medicine, the app says so instead of inventing an answer.

OR JUST TYPE THE NAME
Spell it wrong and it still finds it. "Panadooll" finds paracetamol. Suggestions appear as you type.

AN ANSWER YOU CAN ACTUALLY READ
What it is for. How to take it. Food and drink. What to do if you miss a dose. How to store it. Short sentences, clear headings, one idea per line — written the way a pharmacist would explain it across a counter, not the way a leaflet is printed.

SIDE EFFECTS, SORTED BY HOW URGENT THEY ARE
Three separate lists: the common and usually mild ones, the ones worth calling your doctor about, and the ones that mean stop and get help today. No symptom appears twice with two different urgencies.

FULL ARABIC, NOT A TRANSLATED MENU
The whole interface mirrors right to left, set in proper Arabic type — and the answer itself is in Arabic, not just the headings. Switch language at any time, on any screen, without searching again.

KEEP YOUR MEDICINES, AND YOUR FAMILY'S
Save a medicine and it stays readable with no internet at all. Keep separate lists for separate people — yourself, a parent, a child. If two saved medicines share an active ingredient, the app warns you, because that is exactly how a paracetamol dose gets accidentally doubled.

REMINDERS THAT ACTUALLY GO OFF
Set the times you take something and your phone will tell you, even after a restart.

A PROFESSIONAL VIEW
Pharmacists, pharmacy students and prescribers get a second view of the same medicine: ATC code, pharmacological class, indications, mechanism of action, absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, half-life, contraindications, interactions grouped by mechanism, adverse effects grouped by organ system, and what to monitor.

SHARE IT OR KEEP IT
Send an answer as text, or export a properly formatted PDF — including in Arabic, with the right fonts embedded.

BUILT TO RESPECT YOU
No account. No sign-up. No password. Your saved medicines, the people they belong to and your reminder times never leave your phone — there is no copy on any server. The app asks for three permissions: internet, network state, and the camera. No location, no contacts, no storage access.

IMPORTANT — PLEASE READ
This app gives information, not medical advice. The information is generated by AI and can be incomplete or wrong. It is here to help you understand a box you are already holding. It cannot examine you, it does not know your history, and it is not a substitute for a pharmacist or a doctor. Always confirm before you take anything, and seek professional help in an emergency.
```
3,127 characters.

## Data safety answers

The form currently declares **Personal info** as collected. Read against the
live privacy policy that is wrong: the name, age and sex a user may type for a
report are stored on the device and never sent anywhere. Declaring collection
that does not happen is its own compliance problem, because Play checks the
form against the policy.

What actually leaves the device, and how to declare it:

| What the app does | Declare | Purpose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sends the typed medicine name to the API | App activity → **Search history** | App functionality | Not linked to any identity — there is no account |
| Sends the first letters for type-ahead | same as above | App functionality | Nothing is stored about who typed them |
| Sends a photo of the pack to be read | Photos and videos → **Photos** | App functionality | Mark it **processed ephemerally** — the policy says it is not stored, by you or by Google |
| Hashes the caller's network address | App info and performance → nothing, or Device IDs if Play insists | **Fraud prevention, security and compliance** | The address itself is never written down; only a salted, irreversible hash plus a timestamp |
| Saved medicines, people, reminder times, language, theme | **Do not declare** | — | Device-only storage is not collection |
| Patient name, age, sex for a report | **Do not declare** | — | Device-only. This is the field that is wrong today |

Answer **yes** to "Is all of the user data collected by your app encrypted in
transit?" — everything goes over HTTPS.

Answer **no** to "Do you provide a way for users to request that their data is
deleted?" only if that is true; there is no account to delete, and uninstalling
removes everything held on the device. Play accepts that, but say it the way
the form asks.

Two of these are judgement calls rather than facts — whether the Gemini API
counts as sharing with a third party (it is a service provider processing on
your behalf, so "not shared" is defensible), and whether a salted hash of an
address needs declaring at all. Read Play's own definition on each screen
before you tick it.
